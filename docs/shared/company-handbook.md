## Finding trustworthy information

Start with the source document linked from a learning step or cited by a knowledge answer. Check the document's scope and last-updated date before applying instructions. If two documents disagree, report the disagreement with links and dates rather than silently treating one as authoritative.

Software and HR documents may both be useful to the same employee. A document's team label describes its subject matter; it does not make the document exclusive to that team. This demo corpus is a common searchable pool.

## Accounts and access

Use your own invited account and keep credentials private. Request missing access from the appropriate administrator. Do not share passwords, access tokens, private keys, or confidential employee records through repository files or company chatbot questions.

An invitation, a team membership, a team-admin appointment, and a Project assignment represent different actions. Being able to review a team's learning material as an administrator does not automatically add that team's employee paths to your own assigned list.

## Learning and support

Follow assigned learning steps and open their source links when additional context is needed. Ask the onboarding buddy for clarification if a step cannot be completed safely with the available information.

If a source refresh fails, an existing path may remain available with a freshness warning. A warning means the saved content may be out of date; report it and verify sensitive instructions with the relevant team contact. Do not invent missing steps or citations.
