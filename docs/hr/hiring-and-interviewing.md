## Approving an open role

Before recruiting begins, the hiring manager submits a role request with the business need, responsibilities, work arrangement, expected start window, and proposed compensation range. The department lead and Finance confirm the position and budget; HR confirms the approval is recorded before publishing the opening.

The hiring manager and HR write a role description that separates essential qualifications from skills that can be learned after joining. Use job-related criteria and describe the actual work. HR maintains the approved posting and handles changes to its scope or compensation range. Interviewers should not promise a different role or package during a conversation.

## Preparing a structured interview plan

Before reviewing applicants, HR and the hiring manager agree on a scorecard covering four to six job-related criteria. For an HR coordinator, criteria might include scheduling accuracy, clear employee communication, handling confidential information, and resolving incomplete requests. Each criterion includes examples of evidence that would support a favorable assessment.

The sample sequence is an HR screening conversation, a role-focused interview, and a collaboration interview. Each stage has an assigned interviewer and a distinct purpose. Use the same core questions and scoring criteria for candidates applying to the same role, with follow-up questions to clarify individual answers.

Interviewers complete the [interviewer preparation training](required-training-and-compliance.md) before participating. Disclose a personal relationship or other conflict to HR before accessing an application. HR decides whether to reassign the interviewer and records that decision privately.

## Scheduling and candidate accommodations

HR sends the candidate the interview format, expected duration, participants' roles, and preparation requirements. Any practical exercise must be agreed in advance, take no more than 60 minutes in this sample process, and use fictional or public information. Do not ask candidates to perform unpaid production work or disclose confidential material from another employer.

Offer a private way to request interview accommodations. HR coordinates adjustments with the candidate and shares only the information interviewers need to run the interview. Ask about availability and ability to perform the role's essential tasks using HR-approved wording; do not request diagnoses or unrelated personal details.

## Conducting interviews and recording evidence

At the start of the interview, explain the agenda and leave time for the candidate's questions. Ask about relevant experience and how the candidate would approach realistic work situations. Avoid questions about family plans, age, religion, medical history, or other personal matters unrelated to the role. If a candidate volunteers sensitive information, redirect to job-related topics without adding that information to evaluation notes.

Enter feedback in the private applicant tracking system within one business day, before reading other interviewers' ratings. Record concrete evidence against each assigned criterion. Distinguish missing evidence from a negative finding, and avoid vague judgments such as whether someone would fit in socially. Do not record interviews or upload candidate material to an external AI tool without an HR-approved process and the required candidate permission.

## Making a decision and issuing an offer

The hiring manager leads a debrief after independent feedback is submitted. Compare evidence against the scorecard rather than ranking candidates by personal preference. HR records the decision and checks unresolved concerns. If evidence is missing, agree on a focused follow-up rather than changing the criteria for one candidate.

HR verifies the approved compensation and any required checks before issuing the written offer. Only HR or an authorized approver communicates a formal offer or changes its terms. HR handles candidate updates and closes unsuccessful applications respectfully. Once an offer is accepted, HR hands the agreed role, team, start date, and onboarding contact to the [employee onboarding process](employee-onboarding.md).

## Protecting candidate information

Applications, interview notes, and offer details stay in the private recruiting system with access limited to the hiring team and authorized approvers. Follow the HR retention schedule; do not create personal archives or delete official records independently. Report a misdirected application or accidental disclosure promptly through the security reporting channel and notify HR privately.
