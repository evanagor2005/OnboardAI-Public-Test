## Paid time off balances

In this sample program, regular full-time employees earn 10 hours of paid time off (PTO) at the end of each full calendar month of employment, for 120 hours in a complete year. PTO covers planned vacation and personal time. The HR system displays the available balance; employees should check it before submitting a request. Part-time schedules and other employment arrangements require HR confirmation of the applicable allowance.

Up to 40 unused PTO hours carry into the next calendar year. The maximum available balance is 160 hours; accrual pauses at that balance and resumes when the balance falls below it. Employees cannot use a negative balance without an exception documented by HR. These sample rules do not determine an employee's actual leave rights or final-pay treatment.

## Requesting planned time off

Submit planned PTO in the HR system at least 10 business days before the first day away when practical. Include the dates, hours requested, and a brief coverage plan. Personal reasons are optional for ordinary PTO requests. A manager checks staffing and responds within three business days; a submitted request is not approved until the system records approval.

Managers consider coverage needs and previously approved absences consistently. If a request cannot be accommodated, the manager explains the coverage conflict and discusses alternative dates. Escalate an unanswered request to HR when the response window has passed. Do not assume silence is approval.

After approval, update the team calendar with availability only, arrange a handoff, and enter the approved hours under the correct [timesheet category](payroll-and-timesheets.md). A shared calendar should not disclose a diagnosis, family situation, or other private leave details. Changes to approved dates require an updated request and manager confirmation.

## Illness and unexpected absences

The sample program provides a separate bank of 40 paid sick hours at the start of each calendar year; new full-time hires receive that bank on their start date. The sample sick bank does not carry forward. HR handles any arrangement that differs from these example rules.

For an unexpected absence, notify the manager before the scheduled start time when possible, or as soon as reasonably practical. State that you are unavailable and when you expect to provide an update. Enter sick time when able; the manager can ask HR to help record an absence if the employee cannot access the system.

A depleted sick balance does not decide whether someone may take leave. HR reviews other available leave arrangements and any applicable protections. Medical evidence, if needed, goes to the private HR channel rather than to a team calendar, repository, or chatbot.

## Extended leave and confidential coordination

Contact HR for parental, medical, caregiving, bereavement, or other extended leave. HR explains the applicable eligibility review, documentation, pay arrangements, benefits treatment, and return-to-work process. This sample guide does not assign a fixed duration or promise paid coverage for those leave types.

HR coordinates necessary scheduling information with the manager while limiting access to sensitive records. The manager plans coverage; HR handles confidential documentation. Before returning, confirm the return date and any agreed workplace adjustments with HR and the manager.

## Holidays and balance corrections

HR publishes the company holiday calendar before the new year. Employees supporting a scheduled service on a holiday confirm coverage and the applicable time category with their manager before the shift. Do not record both worked hours and leave hours for the same time.

Report an incorrect balance to HR with the affected dates and request reference. Payroll corrects a closed pay period through the correction process rather than having the employee change a previously approved record without a review trail.
