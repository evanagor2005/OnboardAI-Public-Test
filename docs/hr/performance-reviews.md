## Review cycle and responsibilities

Cedar Works uses quarterly goal conversations, a June midyear check-in, and a November annual review in this sample process. HR publishes exact deadlines and opens the private review forms. The annual review considers work since the previous annual cycle; a new employee's review covers only the period since their start date.

Employees prepare a self-assessment and discuss development needs. Managers collect relevant evidence, write the assessment, and hold the conversation. HR coordinates consistency and handles process concerns. The onboarding buddy may help a new employee understand the process but does not receive access to the employee's review by default.

## Setting goals and expectations

At the start of a quarter, the employee and manager agree on three to five goals appropriate to the role. Each goal identifies an expected outcome, a way to assess progress, a target date, and relevant dependencies. For example, an HR coordinator might aim to complete each assigned new-hire access checklist by the employee's first day and review any missed handoffs each month.

Record goals in the private review system and revisit them during regular check-ins. If priorities change, document the new expectation and why it changed. Assess performance against the expectations that applied during the period rather than an undocumented goal introduced at review time.

## Preparing the self-assessment and feedback

Employees submit a self-assessment at least five business days before the annual review meeting. Summarize outcomes, examples of collaboration, obstacles, lessons learned, and requested support. Include enough context to explain an achievement without copying candidate records, payroll information, or other confidential material into the review.

Managers may request focused feedback from colleagues who worked directly with the employee. Feedback should describe observable actions and their impact, not rumors or personal characteristics. Do not promise anonymity: HR limits access, but relevant feedback may need to be discussed so the employee can understand and respond to it.

## Assessing performance consistently

The sample rating scale is developing, meeting expectations, and exceeding expectations. Developing means important agreed expectations are not yet met consistently. Meeting expectations means the employee reliably delivers the agreed role outcomes. Exceeding expectations requires sustained evidence of impact beyond the agreed outcomes, not simply longer working hours.

Managers explain each assessment with examples from the full review period. HR facilitates a consistency review before assessments are finalized, using the minimum employee information needed. Ratings should account for changed priorities and available resources. Approved leave or an agreed accommodation is not, by itself, evidence of poor performance; managers consult HR about questions involving those circumstances.

## Holding the review conversation

The manager shares the written draft at least two business days before the meeting. The conversation covers achievements, gaps, employee feedback, and a practical development plan. Choose one or two development actions with an owner, target date, and follow-up date. Required learning can be tracked using the [training process](required-training-and-compliance.md).

The employee acknowledges receiving the final review in the system. Acknowledgment confirms receipt, not agreement. Employees can add a written response and request an HR discussion if evidence is inaccurate or the process appears inconsistent. HR explains the review outcome and records any correction without deleting the original review history.

## Follow-up and access to records

The manager checks progress on agreed actions during regular one-to-one meetings. Significant performance concerns should be raised promptly with HR support rather than saved as a surprise for the annual review. Any formal improvement process is handled separately with clear expectations and documented follow-up.

Review records are available to the employee, their authorized manager, and designated HR staff. A review does not automatically create a promotion, compensation change, or disciplinary action. HR communicates those decisions through their separate processes. Do not place review records in a shared learning path or the public sample repository.
